For most Bangalore brides, the wedding outfit gets decided months in advance — but bridal bangles are often an afterthought squeezed into the final two weeks. That's a mistake. Bridal bangles Bangalore photographers will tell you are one of the most captured details of the entire wedding, appearing in every close-up from the mehendi hand to the muhurtham mandap.

Getting the right bridal bangle set means thinking about it as early as you think about your saree or lehenga. This guide walks through everything a Bangalore bride needs to know before buying bridal bangles — the types available, how to match them to your outfit and ceremony, sizing, and how many sets you actually need.

Why Bridal Bangles Matter More Than You Think

In South Indian and Kannada wedding traditions, bangles aren't just an accessory — they're part of the visual language of the ceremony. A muhurtham look calls for a heavier, more traditional stack, while a reception look often leans toward sleeker, more contemporary designs.

Photographers frequently build entire shot sequences around hand gestures during rituals like the mangalsutra-tying or the exchange of garlands, which means your wedding bangles Bangalore album will feature your wrist stack front and center — whether you planned for it or not.

Types of Bridal Bangles to Consider

Kundan Bangles

Kundan work involves setting uncut, polished glass or stones into gold-toned metal using a traditional foiling technique. It's the most recognizable "bridal" look in North and South Indian weddings alike, and it pairs naturally with red, maroon, and gold outfits. Kundan bangles for wedding ceremonies tend to be broader and more ornate, making them the strongest choice for your main wedding day. At Sri Kannika Bangles, our master craftsmen specialize in heritage Kundan work refined over three decades.

Jadau Bangles

Jadau is closely related to Kundan but typically features more intricate metalwork and a slightly heavier, more regal finish — historically associated with royal Rajasthani craftsmanship. If you want a bridal look that reads as more elaborate and ceremonial, Jadau bangles are worth considering for your muhurtham or wedding day set.

AD Stone (American Diamond) Bangles

AD stone bangles use cubic zirconia set into silver-toned or gold-toned metal for a sparkle closer to real diamonds. These work beautifully for sangeet and reception looks, especially with pastel or contemporary outfits where you want shine without the heavier gold-toned look of Kundan. Browse our full AD stone and Kundan bangles collection to see both finishes side by side.

Micro Gold Plated Bangles

For brides who want a design that reads as solid gold without the weight or cost, micro gold plating over brass or copper gives a bright, long-lasting gold finish. These are a practical choice for daily wear post-wedding, or for smaller functions like haldi where you don't want to risk your heavier bridal bangle set.

Antique Finish Bangles

Antique or oxidized-gold finish bangles have become popular for their temple-jewellery aesthetic — think of the matte, sculpted look often paired with Kanjeevaram sarees. These suit brides going for a more traditional, understated South Indian bridal bangles Bangalore look rather than high-shine Kundan.

Matching Bangles to Your Outfit

Red or Maroon Lehenga / Saree Kundan or Jadau in gold tones — the warmth of the gold complements deep reds.
Pastel or Contemporary (Sangeet / Reception) AD stone or rose-gold tone bangles for a modern sparkle.
Kanjeevaram Silk / Temple Styling Antique finish bangles with temple motifs for a heritage look.
Simple Mehendi or Haldi Outfits Colourful glass or micro gold plated bangles — festive without competing with the wedding-day set.

A general rule when choosing bridal bangles: the more elaborate your outfit's embroidery, the simpler your bangles should be, and vice versa. If your lehenga is heavily embellished, a broad ornate Kundan stack can look overwhelming — a slightly narrower design lets the outfit lead.

Bangle Sizing: Don't Guess This Part

Bangle sizing is measured by the internal diameter, usually in inches (commonly 2.4" to 2.8" for adult women). The simplest way to size at home: take a bangle you already wear comfortably, measure the internal diameter with a ruler, and use that as your reference.

If you're between sizes, size up slightly — swelling from mehendi application and long wear during ceremonies is common, and bangles that are too tight become uncomfortable within a few hours.

How Many Bangle Sets Do You Actually Need?

Most Bangalore weddings involve at least three to four separate functions, and brides often underestimate how many bridal bangle sets they need across all of them:

  1. Haldi / Mehendi: Colourful glass or lightweight bangles — comfort matters most here since you'll be wearing them for hours during mehendi application.
  2. Sangeet: AD stone or contemporary designs that photograph well under stage lighting.
  3. Muhurtham / Wedding Day: Your heaviest, most traditional Kundan or Jadau set — this is the one that appears in your main wedding photographs.
  4. Reception: A sleeker, often more Western-leaning set that complements a gown or fusion outfit.

Buying pieces individually as each function approaches is more stressful than planning your full bangle wardrobe in one visit. It also lets a jeweller advise on how the sets will look together across your wedding album, rather than each piece being chosen in isolation. Caring for Handcrafted Bridal Bangles

Kundan, Jadau, and AD stone work are handcrafted using foiling and stone-setting techniques that don't respond well to moisture or harsh chemicals. A few basics to preserve your wedding bangles Bangalore collection:

  • Remove bangles before applying perfume, hairspray, or moisturizer.
  • Store each set separately in a soft pouch to prevent stones from scratching against each other.
  • Avoid direct sunlight for long periods, which can dull the foil behind Kundan stones.
  • Have any loose stones re-set promptly rather than continuing to wear a piece with a missing stone — moisture and dust can get underneath and affect the setting.

Final Thoughts

Bridal bangles deserve the same planning time as your outfit, not a rushed decision the week before your wedding. Start with your muhurtham outfit's colour palette, work outward to your other functions, and try to see multiple designs in person if possible — the play of light on Kundan and AD stone work is difficult to judge from photos alone.

If you're shopping for bridal bangles in Bangalore, visiting a store that specializes in handcrafted pieces — rather than mass-produced stock — makes it easier to get sizing right and see the full range of finishes side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. How far in advance should I buy bridal bangles?

Ideally 2-3 months before the wedding, especially if you want a custom size or a specific design made to order. This also gives time for exchanges if sizing needs adjustment.

Q. Can I wear the same bangles for multiple ceremonies?

You can, but most brides prefer different sets for different functions since the outfit style and lighting vary significantly between haldi, sangeet, and the main wedding day.

Q. What's the difference between Kundan and Jadau bangles?

Kundan uses polished stones set into gold-toned metal with a foiling technique, while Jadau typically involves more elaborate, dense metalwork and is associated with a heavier, more regal finish. Both are popular for bridal wear, and the choice usually comes down to how ornate you want the design.

Q. Are AD stone bangles durable?

Yes, when handled with basic care — avoiding water, perfume, and rough handling — AD stone bangles hold their shine well and are a popular choice for repeat wear at multiple functions, not just the wedding day.
