Ask five brides which bridal bangle style is "the best," and you'll likely get five different answers depending on their outfit, budget, and the ceremony they're dressing for.

Kundan, Jadau, and AD Stone are the three styles that come up most often in Bangalore bridal shopping — and while they can look similar in photos, they're genuinely different in craftsmanship, cost, and how they wear over the course of a long wedding day.

Here's a clear, practical comparison to help you decide for your upcoming wedding celebrations.

What Is Kundan Jewellery?

Kundan is one of the oldest jewellery-making techniques in India, dating back centuries to royal courts in Rajasthan and Gujarat. The process involves setting polished, uncut glass or gemstones into gold-toned metal using a foiling technique that gives the stones their glow from behind, rather than relying on faceted cuts to catch light.

  • Look: Warm, gold-toned, slightly matte glow rather than sharp sparkle.
  • Best for: Traditional wedding-day and muhurtham looks, red/maroon/gold outfits.
  • Typical price range: Mid to premium, depending on stone quality and metalwork detail.

What Is Jadau Jewellery?

Jadau is closely related to Kundan — in fact, Jadau refers to the technique of embedding stones directly into gold using minimal or no glue, while Kundan often refers more specifically to the foiled-stone finish. In practice, Jadau pieces tend to be denser, more elaborately worked, and read as more "regal" than standard Kundan designs, often featuring more intricate base metalwork visible around the stones.

  • Look: Heavier, more ornate, more visible metalwork detail than Kundan.
  • Best for: Brides wanting a more elaborate, statement bridal-day look; pairs well with heavily embroidered lehengas and traditional silk sarees.
  • Typical price range: Generally on the higher end of the handcrafted bangle spectrum due to the density of metalwork.

What Is AD Stone (American Diamond) Jewellery?

AD stone jewellery uses cubic zirconia — a diamond-look-alike stone — set into silver-toned or gold-toned metal. Unlike the warm, foiled glow of Kundan, AD stones are faceted to catch and reflect light, giving a brighter, more diamond-like sparkle.

  • Look: Bright, high-shine, closer to fine jewellery sparkle than the matte warmth of Kundan.
  • Best for: Sangeet, reception, and contemporary or pastel outfits where you want maximum shine under stage or event lighting.
  • Typical price range: Generally more accessible than premium Kundan or Jadau work, making it a popular choice for multiple functions.

Which One Should You Actually Choose?

If you're dressing for your main muhurtham or wedding ceremony in a traditional red or maroon outfit, Kundan or Jadau will complement the gold tones of a classic bridal look far better than AD stone's cooler, brighter sparkle.

If you're planning your sangeet or reception look, especially in a lighter or more contemporary outfit, AD stone bangles tend to photograph beautifully under event lighting without the heavier visual weight of Kundan or Jadau.

If comfort across a long day matters — and for most brides, it does — AD stone is generally lighter to wear for extended hours than dense Jadau work.

If budget is a factor across multiple functions, many brides choose one premium Kundan or Jadau set for the main wedding day and fill in sangeet, haldi, and smaller functions with AD stone or micro gold plated pieces from our bangles collection, which are more affordable to buy in multiples.

Durability: What to Expect From Each

All three styles are handcrafted and benefit from the same basic care — avoiding water, perfume, and rough handling. Kundan and Jadau, because stones are set using foiling and traditional embedding rather than modern claw settings, are more sensitive to moisture getting behind the stone, which can dull the foil over time. AD stone settings are generally more secure for regular wear since the stones are set more like conventional fashion jewellery.

None of the three should be treated as everyday wear — even the most durable AD stone bangle is designed for occasion wear, not daily use.

Final Thoughts

There's no single "best" between Kundan, Jadau, and AD stone — the right choice depends entirely on which ceremony you're dressing for and what outfit it needs to sit alongside. Seeing all three finishes in person under different lighting makes the difference far more obvious than comparing photos online.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is Jadau more expensive than Kundan?

Generally yes — Jadau's denser metalwork and more elaborate craftsmanship typically place it at the premium end, though pricing varies by design complexity and stone quality across both styles.

Q. Can AD stone bangles be worn for the main wedding ceremony?

Yes, though most brides pair AD stone with sangeet or reception looks and reserve Kundan or Jadau for the main muhurtham, since the warmer gold tones traditionally complement red and maroon bridal outfits better.

Q. Which is lighter to wear — Kundan or AD stone?

AD stone bangles are typically lighter, making them a more comfortable choice for functions where you'll be wearing bangles for many hours, such as sangeet performances.

Q. Do Kundan and Jadau bangles tarnish over time?

With proper care — avoiding moisture, perfume, and direct sunlight for extended periods — the gold-toned finish holds up well, though like all handcrafted foiled jewellery, it should be stored separately and handled gently.
